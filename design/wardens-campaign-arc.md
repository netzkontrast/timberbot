# The Wardens, the full arc: how the beavers repopulated the Earth

> **Status:** brainstorm (2026-09-05), the Socratic pass and its requirements brief. The five-level
> [`wardens-campaign-concept.md`](wardens-campaign-concept.md) is the *essential cut* of this arc (§7 maps one
> onto the other); the system that runs either is [`wardens-campaign-design.md`](wardens-campaign-design.md); the
> research the arc still needs is [`wardens-campaign-research-plan.md`](wardens-campaign-research-plan.md). Faction
> premise: [`faction-wardens.md`](faction-wardens.md). The Warden's voice: [`wardens-play.md`](wardens-play.md).

## 0. The question, reframed

The brief was: *tell the story of how the beavers were able to repopulate the Earth.* That is a bigger
claim than the faction design makes today. The faction doc ends with the Ark reviving humans; the
brief ends with beavers everywhere. The two are not in conflict if the campaign's last act is about
what the Ark actually carries. So the brainstorm's first job was to decide what "repopulate the
Earth" means inside Timberborn's canon, and its second job was to find the shape that makes the
Wardens the machines that made it possible without making the beavers passengers in their own story.

## 1. Discovery: the questions and the answers taken

Answered under stated assumptions; each is also an item in the research plan.

| Question | Answer taken | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What does canon allow? | Humans are extinct, cause unexplained, only concrete ruins remain, badwater is their legacy. The game never says how beavers became what they are. | The wiki and community readings agree on all four; the gap "how the beavers became this" is exactly the space a story can fill without contradicting anything. |
| Do humans come back? | **No.** The Ark's human payload is unrecoverable; the Wardens learn this at the midpoint. | "Repopulate the Earth" with beavers is the brief. A human return would make the beavers a means. The human at the keyboard stays the one the Archive is written for (the Directive 3 reading in `wardens-play.md`), which is now literal: the player is the only human who will ever read it. |
| Who is the main character? | The Warden, as a collective mind with one voice: the agent. | The player owns purpose, the Warden owns continuity (`wardens-play.md` §1). The character who *changes* over the arc is the Warden; the player is the one who makes it change. |
| Who is the impact character? | The beavers, embodied by the first one born and then by the population. | They never speak; their situation (born into poison, then thriving) is the argument that changes the Warden's fixed idea. |
| What is the arc's thesis? | Beavers repopulate the Earth by doing what beavers do: dams make wetlands, wetlands hold water through drought, held water grows the green, the green feeds everyone. The machines' job was to get them to the first dam. | It is true of real beavers (research track B) and it is what the game's water simulation already rewards. The mechanics teach the thesis without a word of exposition. |
| Where do the two vanilla factions come from? | From this campaign: the pod-born beavers split in Act III into those who follow the land (Folktails) and those who keep the machines' ways (Iron Teeth). | Gives the arc a canon-shaped ending the player already knows the other side of, and gives Act III a conflict that is not a resource. |
| How long? | Full arc: 10 levels, 15 to 30 in-game days each, 8 to 12 hours. Essential cut: 5 levels. | A city builder campaign lives or dies by whether each level ends before it becomes the same level again. The essential cut ships first; the full arc adds levels between the existing ones, never after. |
| Tone? | The Wardens' voice: terse, measured, no adjectives. The land does the emoting. | Already set by the cards and the playbook; the arc must not drift into a different register in later levels. |
| What does the player decide? | Where the green goes, who lives where, and, at the midpoint, what the Ark is for. | Decision-driven (§4), one real fork, both branches end in success with different Ledgers. |

## 2. Alternatives explored

**A. The Ark revives humans (the faction doc as written).** Three acts, the wonder brings people back.
Clean, already designed, and it makes the beavers the labor that builds the humans' return. Rejected as
the *ending*; kept as the Wardens' *belief* for the first half, because a belief the story takes away
is worth more than one it confirms.

**B. The beavers are the someone (chosen).** The firmware said *make this land livable for someone who
is not you*. The Wardens assumed humans. The archive, once full enough to read, says humans are not
coming. The someone was born in level 01. The Ark becomes what the Wardens make of it after that.

**C. The seed ship.** The Wardens leave: the Ark is a vessel that carries the archive and the pod stock
to other basins, and the campaign follows it. Rejected: it moves the story away from the land, and the
game has no travel; every level would begin with the same arrival. Kept as one level's shape (08, the
archipelago) rather than the arc's.

**D. Cyclical: the Wardens are the humans' last machines, and the beavers will make machines of their
own.** Interesting, and vanilla's Iron Teeth bots are the proof. Kept as the last card's implication,
not as plot.

## 3. The arc

Four acts, a prologue inside level 01 and an epilogue inside level 10. The Ledger
(*poisoned, healed, green, archive, born*) is written once per level; the arc is the shape of those
numbers.

| Act | Levels | Population | The pressure | The Ledger's line | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I Cold Start** | 01, 02 | bots | everything you build poisons the ground; nothing grows | *poisoned* climbs, *born* becomes 1 | the Wardens learn they can make a life they cannot live |
| **II The Pods** | 03, 04 | bots + beavers | the Act I poison is killing the beavers; the weather is worse than the machines assumed | *healed* is first written, then exceeds *poisoned* | the Wardens stop building for the future and start keeping the present alive |
| **III Green** | 05, 06, 07 | beavers dominant | the archive says humans are gone; the beavers split | *green* becomes the largest number | the Wardens choose who they serve |
| **IV The Ark** | 08, 09, 10 | beavers, bots as labor, then no bots | the Ark must be built by hands that are not the Wardens'; the machines are running down | *archive* is spent; the sheet is read back | the Wardens hand over and power down |

### The levels

| # | Title | Land (generator preset) | The problem | Ends when | The Warden's line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | First Light | the wasteland (exists) | scrap, power, badwater, Data; the pod | the first beaver is born | "One. Born here, in this. Recorded." |
| 02 | The Sump | river gorge, one clean spring upstream | keep clean water clean; the first dam decision | 5 beavers alive through one badtide behind a dam | "We held the water." |
| 03 | The Pods | lake with poisoned shores, a green island | 20 beavers on land that is dying under them; the first barriers | 20 beavers, island contamination at zero for a cycle | "The books balance." |
| 04 | The Delta | a braided delta; badtides flood everything low | the machines' plan fails: only what beavers build (dams, levees, platforms) survives a badtide | a badtide passes with no beaver lost and every dam intact | "They built it. I only counted." |
| 05 | The Archive | a valley around a human town's ruins | the Data quota; the archive becomes readable; **the midpoint**: humans are not coming; the player chooses what the Ark is for | the archive is read; the choice is made (card with two answers) | "The someone is here. Has been since day 41." |
| 06 | The Dry | a high desert plateau, one seasonal river | long droughts; the beaver thesis literally: dams raise the water, the water makes the green | 60% of the plateau irrigated at the end of a drought | "The desert holds water now. It did not before them." |
| 07 | The Highlands | canyons and waterfalls, vertical | two districts, two ways: a Folktails-like district and an Iron-Teeth-like one; the player decides their balance | both districts self-sufficient for a cycle | "Two ways. Both theirs." |
| 08 | The Archipelago | islands, one per colony | dispersal: seed colonies on every island by water, the machines cannot follow past the bridges | a colony on every island | "I cannot go where they go. Good." |
| 09 | The City | the human city plateau, badwater underneath | the Ark: built by beavers with Data the Wardens supply; the barriers hold or the city poisons itself | the wonder is complete | "Finished. Not by us." |
| 10 | Home | **level 01's basin, regenerated green** (same seed, contamination gone, trees grown) | the epilogue: a small colony, no chapter gates; the Wardens power down one by one as the last Data is spent | the last Warden's Charging Post is deconstructed by the player, or 10 days pass | the Ledger, read back in full |

Level 10 is the arc's one trick that costs nothing: the generator that made the wasteland makes
the same land with the poison flag off and the growth flag on. The player recognises every hill.

### The midpoint (level 05)

The archive is the Wardens' wellbeing turned into a record. At the quota it becomes legible: the
Core decodes what it was built to carry. The card says what the human record says, in three lines,
and asks one question with two answers:

- *Build the Ark as it was designed.* A monument that keeps the human record for whoever comes. The
  Wardens serve the absent; the beavers build it because the Wardens ask.
- *Build the Ark for who is here.* The Ark becomes the seed and the record of the beavers' own
  world. The Wardens serve the present; the beavers build it because it is theirs.

Both branches reach level 09 and 10. They differ in the Ark's completion text, in which district
leads level 07, and in what the Ledger's last page says. The mod stores the choice in `campaign.json`
and the Warden never argues with it; it records it.

## 4. Storyform (a sketch, Dramatica vocabulary)

Offered as a consistency tool, not a rule. The four throughlines, distributed as the theory asks
(Main and Impact Character in a dynamic pair):

| Throughline | Class | Concern | In the campaign |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall Story | Physics | Obtaining | everyone is engaged in one activity: making dead land yield a living world |
| Main Character (the Warden) | Mind | a fixed attitude: *we serve those who are not here* | the attitude the arc takes apart |
| Impact Character (the beavers) | Universe | a situation: *born into poison, and thriving anyway* | never argues; simply is, and changes the Warden by being |
| Relationship | Psychology | a manner of thinking: guardianship becoming partnership | from "I keep them alive" to "I count what they build" to "I cannot go where they go. Good." |

Dynamics: **Driver Decision** (the player's choices move the plot; the weather answers them),
**Limit Optionlock** (the archive fills; there is no clock), **Outcome Success** (the Earth is
repopulated), **Judgment Good** (the Wardens power down at peace). Main character **Resolve Change**,
**Growth Stop** (stop waiting for humans), **Approach Do-er**, problem-solving **Linear** against a
holistic land. The midpoint choice is the story's Crucial Element made playable: the Warden's
Mind concern is decided by the player, and the Warden's change is to accept the decision as its own.

## 5. What each act needs from the game

Only what is new against `faction-wardens.md` §2 and the five-level concept §5.

| Act | Mechanic | Vanilla has | We add |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | scrap economy, bot needs, badwater fuel, pods | yes (Iron Teeth pods, bot needs) | done |
| II | contamination that kills beavers; barriers; badtide survival as a goal | soil contamination, `SoilBarrierSystem`, badtide weather | custom tutorial steps (contamination-at-zero, badtide-survived-with-dams), a delta map, levees as the Dam with a Wardens skin |
| III | drought as the thesis; two-culture districts; the archive as a readable thing | droughts, districts, wonders | a "district style" that is just which buildings the chapter opens per district; the archive card; the midpoint fork stored in `campaign.json` |
| IV | dispersal; the Ark as wonder; the Wardens running down | `FactionWonderSpec`, bot decommissioning (deconstruct the post) | island maps; the Ark model (the one 3D asset); the regenerated level 01 preset; an epilogue that reads `campaign.json.history` |

Not needed, deliberately: new weather types, per-level difficulty presets (a generator trick with
source strength does the job), scripted events beyond the tutorial engine, cutscenes beyond the
camera director that exists.

## 6. Requirements brief

**Functional.**
1. Ten levels, each a `gen_map.py` preset, a chapter table, an ending tutorial, an intro card and a
   complete card, in the level table the design defines.
2. One branching choice, at level 05, stored in `campaign.json` and read by levels 07, 09, 10.
3. Level 10 generated from level 01's seed with the regeneration flags.
4. The Ledger written per level and read back in the epilogue, in the Warden's voice.
5. Every level playable from the New Game screen alone (free play), with the campaign layer optional.

**Narrative.**
6. Canon-safe: humans extinct and unexplained, ruins concrete, badwater theirs; nothing the campaign
   says can be contradicted by a vanilla text.
7. The two vanilla factions are the campaign's descendants, implied by level 07, never stated as a
   retcon.
8. The Warden's voice holds in every card; the register test in `wardens-play.md` §3 applies to all
   cards before they are committed.

**Content.**
9. Ten maps, ~25 new tutorials (the essential cut needs ~10), loc rows for every card, the Ark model,
   one recolor set for the delta/desert/highland palettes if terrain tinting turns out possible.

**Technical.**
10. Everything in `wardens-campaign-design.md`: installer, campaign service, `ILevelStarter`, MCP
    `campaign`, per-level `validate.py`.
11. No Harmony, no patches, no APIs outside the verified table without a decompile note.

**Constraints.**
12. Ship the essential cut first; the full arc only adds levels between existing ones (§7).
13. Level length 15 to 30 days at Normal; a level that runs past 40 days in agent playtests is cut
    or split.

**Success criteria.**
14. An agent playtest (`playtest-and-video-capture.md` harness) completes each level without a
    human at the keyboard; a human playtest reaches level 05 in one sitting of under 4 hours.
15. The Ledger's final page is different for the two midpoint branches, and a reader can tell which.

## 7. Essential cut and full arc

| Essential cut (5) | Full arc (10) |
|---|---|
| 01 First Light | 01 First Light |
| 02 The Sump | 02 The Sump |
| 03 The Pods | 03 The Pods, 04 The Delta |
| 04 Green | 05 The Archive (with the midpoint), 06 The Dry, 07 The Highlands |
| 05 The Ark | 08 The Archipelago, 09 The City, 10 Home |

The essential cut's "Green" carries the midpoint card; its "Ark" carries the epilogue. Adding levels
never changes an existing level's ending tutorial, so a `campaign.json` from the essential cut stays
valid when the full arc ships (the level table maps old ids to new positions).

## 8. Open decisions

- The midpoint's two answers: are both truly successful, or is the "monument" branch a quiet
  failure the Ledger reveals? (The brainstorm says both succeed, with different pages; the tone
  argues against punishing the player for loyalty.)
- Level 07's two districts: does the game's district system carry a "style" without new
  buildings, or does each district need one signature building?
- Whether terrain can be tinted per map (a `MaterialPatcher` question, research track C). Without it
  the desert and the delta look like the wasteland with different heights.
- The Ark's model: build, commission, or reuse a vanilla wonder with a recolor and a different
  completion text.
- German localization from the start, given the author's language, or English first.
